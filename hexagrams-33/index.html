Retreat

Strategic withdrawal, boundaries, timing

Heaven over Mountain · Traditional trigram arrangement

Retreat is a deliberate movement away from conditions that cannot be productively engaged. It is not cowardice when it preserves strength and prevents unnecessary entanglement. Distance can restore perspective and create better options.

Core Meaning

Retreat is a deliberate movement away from conditions that cannot be productively engaged. It is not cowardice when it preserves strength and prevents unnecessary entanglement. Distance can restore perspective and create better options.

Recognise when conditions are unfavourable, withdraw respectfully and protect your resources. In work, leave unproductive disputes; in relationships, set boundaries where dialogue has become harmful. Avoid using retreat to evade every difficult responsibility.

Guidance for the Present Situation

Recognise when conditions are unfavourable, withdraw respectfully and protect your resources. In work, leave unproductive disputes; in relationships, set boundaries where dialogue has become harmful. Avoid using retreat to evade every difficult responsibility.

Consider what is actually happening rather than what you fear or hope will happen. Separate facts from assumptions, identify the part you can influence and choose an action proportionate to the circumstances. This hexagram is most useful when its symbolism becomes a practical question about conduct, timing and responsibility.

Work, Decisions and Relationships

Strategic withdrawal, boundaries, timing can be applied to professional choices, personal commitments and relationships by examining how people, resources and circumstances interact. Look for the pattern described by the hexagram rather than treating it as a literal forecast.

In work, review goals, capacity, timing and the effect of your decisions on others. In relationships, consider trust, boundaries, reciprocity and communication. A balanced interpretation should include both opportunity and risk.

Questions for Reflection

1. Which part of this hexagram most closely describes my present circumstances?

2. What is within my influence, and what requires patience, support or acceptance?

3. What is one clear action I can take without forcing an outcome?

About this interpretation

A complete consultation also considers the question asked, any changing lines and the relating hexagram.
